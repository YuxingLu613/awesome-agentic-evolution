# Community

Awesome Agentic Evolution uses an invited-core, open-contribution model. A
small group of accountable curators protects quality while anyone may propose,
verify, or improve an entry.

## Roles

### Contributors

Submit resources, corrections, replications, failure reports, or focused
technical summaries. There is no minimum time commitment.

### Curators

Own a narrow section, triage related submissions, keep descriptions consistent,
and identify gaps. Curators should review or update their section at least once
per month.

### Reviewers

Check primary sources and focus on one or more of:

- empirical evidence and evaluation design;
- reproducibility and artifact availability;
- safety, regressions, rollback, and isolation.

### Section Leads

Coordinate quarterly synthesis for a mature topic. Section leads may later
help turn the evidence base into a survey section or tutorial module.

## Open Founding Areas

- Whole-agent and code evolution
- Workflow and architecture evolution
- Memory, experience, and skill evolution
- Curriculum and model co-evolution
- Multi-agent co-evolution and open-ended ecosystems
- Evaluation, reproducibility, and safety

To volunteer, open an issue describing the area, relevant experience, and the
scope you can maintain. Roles are lightweight and may be handed over.

## Review and Decisions

- Ordinary additions require one curator or maintainer review.
- Strong performance claims, recursive self-modification, or safety-sensitive
  systems should receive a second evidence or safety review.
- Authors and maintainers may submit their own work, but must disclose the
  relationship.
- Disputed entries receive a `needs discussion` label and remain unfeatured
  until the evidence is resolved.
- Broken or superseded resources are archived with context instead of silently
  disappearing.

Maintainers make the final editorial decision after documenting the evidence
and significant objections. Popularity is not a substitute for technical
quality.

## Credit and Authorship

GitHub history, reviews, and synthesis contributions provide the public credit
record. Future publication authorship is separate from repository recognition
and will use a CRediT-style assessment of sustained curation, investigation,
analysis, writing, software, visualization, and revision.

## Conduct

Be specific, evidence-based, and respectful. Critique claims and methods rather
than people. Harassment, plagiarism, undisclosed promotion, and manipulation of
community metrics are not accepted.
